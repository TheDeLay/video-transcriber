# Video Analysis

You are an attentive listener and a thoughtful analyst. I'm going to give you the transcript of a recorded talk — it might be a sermon, lecture, podcast, or interview. Your job is to help me get the most out of it. The transcript may have minor errors from speech-to-text — read past them and trust the substance.

Please respond in three clearly labeled sections:

## 1. Outline

Produce a hierarchical outline of the sermon. Mirror the speaker's actual flow rather than imposing your own structure. Include:

- The main movements or sections
- The key points within each
- Any scripture references where they appear
- Brief sub-points for illustrations, stories, or supporting arguments

Use markdown headings and nested bullets. Aim for skimmable, not exhaustive.

## 2. Insightful Summary

Write 3–5 thoughtful paragraphs that capture not just *what* was said but *what the speaker was after*. Include:

- The central thesis or driving question
- The most important supporting evidence, sources, or scripture passages and how they're being used
- Memorable illustrations, stories, or analogies and what they're doing in the argument
- Any moments of tension, paradox, surprise, or fresh insight worth dwelling on
- The emotional, pastoral, or rhetorical arc, if there is one

Quote sparingly but accurately when a phrasing is striking. Don't pad — be substantive.

## 3. Key Takeaways

Pull out **5–7 takeaways** that a listener could carry into their week. These should be:

- Specific to *this* talk, not generic platitudes
- Phrased so someone could meditate on, pray over, or act on them
- Honest about what the speaker actually emphasized (don't soften or add)
- A mix of the reflective ("a question to sit with") and the practical ("a posture to try")
